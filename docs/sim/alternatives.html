Ten alternative main views

Ten different ideas for the one screen, not ten arrangements of two bars. Every one carries the same checklist — both windows, which one binds, both reset moments, pace, the headline ladder, extra usage in all three of its states, whether the data is real, liveness, failure, the dying-battery warning, and a way into the menu — or says where it dropped something and why. Drawn through lgfx.js with the device's own glyph data, so every string measures what it will measure on the panel. Nothing here is committed to screens.js or src/ui.cpp; the reasoning and the coverage table are in PLAN-dashboard-alternatives.md.

The ten, live

The shipped dashboard is first, for comparison. Drag the sliders and watch which layouts hold their shape and which twitch. The pace thresholds, the pace words, the diamond, the cross, the smoke overlay and the control column are all called out of screens.js rather than re-typed, so a change to the shipped vocabulary moves all eleven screens at once.

Hard state · nearly out

8% left, the sub-12% warning word — 116px in the shipped layout's 102px slot

The state the shipped screen has a known text-fitting problem in. Look for a slot wide enough to hold a warning without shrinking the type, and for whether the headline ladder's swap from a percentage to a clock still reads as the same screen.

Hard state · blocked, with a reason

extra usage rejected, payment_failed on the wire — the longest string the screen ever holds, at the worst moment

Three facts arrive together that no other state carries: a status word, a monthly reset that shares no edge with either window, and a free-text reason. The layouts that treat time as the organising axis have somewhere to put the orphan reset; the ones built on the geometry of a window's edges do not.

Hard state · stale behind a failing refresh

good numbers, 14m old, every attempt since has failed

The failure this project exists to make visible. The countdown ring carries the standing condition as a colour, but the layout still owes a place to say the numbers are old — and that place cannot be the numbers.

The pile-up

8% left, on battery at 6%, blocked with a reason, stale behind a failing refresh

Everything bad at once, which is the only test that separates a layout with room for bad news from one with room for a single piece of it. None of these draws the battery warning as smoke: the overlay's own rule is that it scales with how much of the screen it invalidates, and a dying battery invalidates nothing — the numbers behind it are exactly as true as they were a minute ago. What it invalidates is the screen's future, which earns something permanent rather than something modal. Each layout answers in its own vocabulary, and the answers are deliberately not the same.

Where the battery warning went, layout by layout

Dial
The instrument's bezel. A dial has a rim and nothing else was using it.
Tide
Sediment at the bottom of the tank. No margin exists, so the warning joins the object.
Face
The ahoge wilts and reddens. The only layout where the picture itself can carry it.
Verdict
It takes the one loud slot: the verdict becomes plug in, and the usage verdict demotes to a line. Only when we know the device is on battery — on an unreadable charge state the warning joins the marginalia rather than taking the slot on a guess.
Board
One more row, and the row pitch shrinks to fit it. The cheapest answer of the ten, right up until the table is full.
Agenda
A row at the top, because it is the soonest thing that happens. No runtime is invented: soon is what the gauge honestly supports.
Gap gauge
Bolted to the top edge. A gauge of one relationship has no spare geometry, and anything on the scale would read as part of the measurement.
Grid
The left gutter, which already exists for marks about the field rather than in it. A lit cell would read as a unit of budget.
Horizon
A marked moment on the shared axis, left of both reset ticks — the picture ends before either arrives. Position is nominal and labelled, not measured.
Solo
It takes the top-right line the other window had, and the other window falls back to its strip. The trade Solo claims it can afford, drawn.
