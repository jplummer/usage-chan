Status panel, legend, settings URL

Five approaches each for three screens that do not exist yet. Drawn through lgfx.js and the device's own glyph bitmaps, so every string measures what it will measure on the panel. Nothing here is committed to screens.js or src/ui.cpp; neither file was touched. The controls on the right drive all fifteen at once.

1 · The status panel

Seven readings, one control, two destinations, and a close, on a screen where ui.cpp records that four 46px rows fit 240px and five do not. The crowding is the design problem, and these are five different answers to it rather than five spacings of one.

The one number that decides most of it: 46px is a touch floor, not a layout unit. It is a rule about things you hit. A line of Font2 you only read costs 16px and owes nobody 7mm. So the budget is three targets at 46px and everything else in what is left.

Two things are fixed in every variant. The close control sits at V_CTRL_X, V_CIRCLE_Y0 — exactly where the dashboard's hamburger is — in the same 56x56 zone, so the thing you tapped to get in is the thing you tap to get out, in the same pixels. And brightness is drawBrightnessDots copied, not redesigned: five dots on a 36px pitch, the live one filled.

Degraded is timezone unset, http_529 twice in a row, overage rejected for payment_failed, a weak radio, and the cable out.

Not yet honest

Claude service status
Every variant that draws no service issues is drawing a stub. The device says it without asking anyone — plan.md lists it under Stubs and IOUs, and design-decisions.md has the design it is waiting for: detect with our own request outcomes, name and confirm with status.claude.com's components.json. Until that exists, this line is a lie with a pleasant tone. Pared is the only variant that does not tell it, which is the same claim made by deletion.
battery, and the three states of charging
halBatPercent() returns -1 when the gauge cannot be read, so the unreadable case draws the words and an empty outline with a dash through it — never a partly filled cell that looks like a reading. halIsCharging() returns an enum with an unknown value, so there are three states, not two. On mains with a working gauge, every variant says nothing at all: this device lives plugged in, so the charge level is meaningless almost always and sharply relevant on the day the cable gets knocked out.

2 · The legend

A key for a display that deliberately carries no labels. It has to say what the fill means (what is left), what the bottom edge means (exhaustion and the reset, which are the same edge), what the diamond means, what the colours mean, and what the vessel and its $/X mean.

Real bar is the version plan.md parks, built to settle it rather than argue it. Read the verdict under the grid.

Verdict on the annotated real bar

plan.md says it either works immediately or not at all. It does not work.

Two gutters, 123px on the left and 130px on the right, and Font2 spends 130px in about seventeen characters. Every callout is three or four lines. Three of the five facts — empty, the reset, and where the token sits — are the same edge, so they collapse into one callout with an L-shaped leader; separate captions at y=224 run off the bottom. That is survivable.

What is not survivable is that the bar contradicts the callouts. Switch the state control to degraded: the 5-hour window is at the wall with overage rejected, so the bar is an empty vessel with a red ring, and this much is left, not spent is pointing at nothing, next to the colour says how far ahead of pace you are pointing at no colour. In the healthy state it is the other half that is empty — no token exists, so the $/X callout points at bare background. The legend is incomplete in every state, and wrong in at least one. Verité is the appeal and it is also the defect: a legend has to explain the states you are not in.

Driven by the same state control above. Degraded puts the 5-hour window at the wall with overage rejected, which is the only state in which the real bar has a token to point at.

3 · The settings URL

The device name comes from slugifyHostname(), so the address is http://usage-chan.local — but mDNS fails on plenty of home networks, so the raw IP has to be readable too.

Two facts fix the frame before any variant. LGFXBase::qrcode fills a white box and draws black modules: the palette does not reach it, so a QR is always a white card on the warm black. And its margin argument defaults to false, which leaves only the w / size rounding remainder as a quiet zone. Every call here passes true; the measured result is printed under the grid.

The codes are real and scannable. They use mask 0, declared in the format bits, where the device's own encoder picks the lowest-penalty mask — same version, same module count, same box, different speckle.

Reading this page

why no variant uses 34px rows
34px on this panel is about 5.4mm and missed on hardware; 46px is ~7.3mm and works. The comment above MENU_TOP records it and design-decisions.md repeats it. Shrinking the rows is the one solution to the crowding that is already known to fail.
where the fifth row came from
Pared puts five 46px slots on the screen, which ui.cpp says does not fit. It fits because the version header is gone: 5 x 46 = 230 of 240, and the "tap outside to close" text that used to occupy the top-right corner has been replaced by a close control that sits in the hamburger's own zone.
what the target sizes are
Rows are 46px tall — ~7.3mm — and 246 to 296px wide. Brightness dots own 36 x 46, ~5.7 x 7.3mm, which is ui.cpp's own geometry. Close and back each own 56 x 56, ~8.9 x 8.9mm. The split destination rows are 136 x 46, ~21.6 x 7.3mm.
